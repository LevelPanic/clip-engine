# Clip Engine

YouTube video or Twitch stream → ranked clip candidates → marker-based 9:16 editor → MP4 for Shorts, TikTok and Reels,
plus batch mode (a list of links becomes a queue of rendered, QA'd shorts waiting for review) and stream scanning (a range of a long
or still-live stream, pass after pass).
**What it is for:** an automated clip channel that promotes the creators it clips. Every clip names its creator on screen and every post links back (SCOPE.md → "The idea"; the rule is under "Rules" below).
SCOPE.md is the plan and roadmap. This file covers how to work in the code.
docs/pipeline.md is the end-to-end production pipeline: stream → segments → 3 queue agents (meme, virality, clip-worthiness) → clip extraction/ranking → 9:16 + meme/expert/subtitle passes → user review → schedule.
History: `docs/changelog.md` is the dated log, newest first; `docs/history/` holds the November audits and handovers. Running the channel (the daemons, the scheduler, the watchlist, publishing): `docs/operations.md`.
Resuming work: read SCOPE.md → Roadmap → "Where we left off" (current state) and "On the plate" (the backlog, in priority order, with
where to work and when each item is done) first. Keep both current as you work: tick items off, add what comes up.

## Run

- `./clip <command>` is the CLI (TypeScript through tsx; no build step). `./clip --help` lists every command.
- **Skills:**
  - `/clip <youtube-url> [lens]`: ingest → discover → judge → candidates, then `clip open <id>` opens the Studio on them.
  - `/clip <twitch-link> [--from 1:00:00] [--to …] [--continue]`: scan that range of the stream → judge → candidates.
  - `/queue-check <videoId> --from … --to …`: the full process's first half. Coverage check → transcribe the queue regions once → the three experts in parallel per 20–30 min segment → check every confirm against its stills (`clip queue-review --evidence`) → confirmed moments.
  - `/clip-extractor <videoId>`: confirmed moments → weighted start/end windows → one candidate per moment; no score-based drops.
  - `/clip-curator <videoId>`: niche + fit → keep / review / drop, saved and checked by `clip curate`.
  - `/edit <clipIds|videoId|all>`: trim → layout → editors as proposals → check each → captions → render → QA → submit for review. (`clip-intro` is an optional review pass, only when asked.)
  - `/clip-intro <clipIds>`: write the 4–8 word premise that sits in the hook text; explains the setup, names the person instead of "he", not the punchline. A separate DeepSeek agent, **run only when the user asks for an intro-line review**; not part of the default pipeline.
  - `/subtitle-expert <clipId>`: captions on/off, style/position and word fixes.
  - All live in `.claude/skills/`. `/clip` is the quick pass (one judge on the seeds); the four above are the full process in docs/pipeline.md.
- **Agents:** the three queue experts are subagents in `.claude/agents/` (`meme-expert`, `virality-expert`, `story-expert`); they share `agents/queue-expert.md`. `agents/README.md` is the cast and the contracts, `agents/vocabulary.md` the words (the checked lists print with `./clip vocab`), `agents/research.md` the sources behind the rubrics.
- **Models:** the active model is **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash** (the session and any subagent). Do the small stages inline; delegate only independent, high-volume work to a general-purpose subagent with `model: opencode-go/deepseek-v4.1-flash` and a written, measured brief. Sonnet 5.5 was the Fable-era experiment; its measurements are historical (`docs/history/audit.md` sections 6–10) and it is not used. `agents/README.md` → "Which model runs what" has the current division and the measured lessons.
- **Studio:** `./clip studio` serves http://localhost:4400 (library, candidates, editor, review queue, jobs).
  - `./clip open [videoId|clipId|review]` starts it if needed (detached, log in `.cache/studio.log`) and opens the page.
  - **Candidates page:** Edit (opens the editor) or Export (`POST /api/videos/:id/candidates/export`: as is → render, or with a style → autoedit → render; then QA → review).
  - **Editor export:** renders with `payload.qa`, so QA runs but the review status is left alone, and the layout check reports without switching the layout.
  - **Upload scheduler** (`tools/upload-cron.ts --daemon`): **at most 8 in any 24 hours, 3 hours apart** (`paceAt`, `src/publish/pace.ts`), counted from the publish records. It **stays on one clip until it uploads** (`pickApproved`, `src/review/db.ts`), waiting out YouTube's limits to when they lift; a `careful` period in `channel.json` defers risky approvals (`checkCareful`, `src/qa/unsafe.ts`). **QA does not hold an approval back** (user, 2026-11-22), but a missing credit and the **never-post list** do (see Rules). A broken login holds approvals (`blocked-auth`) until `./clip auth youtube`. Every try is a line in `library/upload-log.jsonl`; `library/upload-status.json` drives the Approved tab's banner. YouTube's channel and project limits, and the history behind each rule: `docs/operations.md` → "Upload scheduler".
  - **Live updates:** the server watches clips/, library/ and exports/ and pushes events over SSE (`/api/events`). Pages batch their reloads (`useDebounced`, 1.5 s): agents save clips several times a second. The review page's video restarts only when its own clip is rendered again. A failed review-list fetch shows the error and retries every 5 s instead of staying on "Loading…".
  - **Sound:** every player (review, editor, candidates) starts muted each time the Studio loads; turning sound on in any of them keeps it on for the visit (`soundOn` in `src/studio/web/api.ts`). The editor has a 🔇/🔊 button (its player has no controls; M already drops a marker).
  - **Review page:** A approve, C changes, R reject then 1–5 for why, U back to review, 8 for 18+, E edit (the editor's ↩ Review comes back to the same clip, `#/review/<clipId>`). On To review the editor's hand-over is shown above the note box, which starts empty: **Make changes needs a note you wrote**, and `setReview` drops a note that is only the hand-over. **C with a note is acted on**: the watchlist queues a `revise` job first in line (`revisionsToQueue`, `src/track/revise.ts`) and the editor agent does what the note asks (`reviseClip`, `src/pipeline/full.ts`). Reasons go to `feedback.jsonl` and `clip stats`. Details: `docs/operations.md` → "Review page".
  - **18+ (2026-11-22, user):** the To review tab has an 18+ check above Approve (`8`); ticked, the clip uploads age-restricted. It starts from QA's 18+ check (`checkAdult`, `src/qa/adult.ts`: words, expert flags, lewd chat, NudeNet on stills; 4 points tick it). `clip adult` runs it again. Details: `docs/operations.md` → "Review page".
- **Batch:** `./clip batch <links | file | channel URL> [--per-video 5] [--lens …] [--style punch] [--reframe]` runs headless.
  - Pipeline: ingest → discover → judge (headless `claude -p`) → make top N → autoedit → render → QA → review queue.
  - `./clip batch-status` shows progress and time per clip.
- **Streams (Twitch):** library ids are `tw-<vod>`. A broadcast is 6–12 h and may still be live, so it's scanned by range.
  - `./clip live [--limit 30]` lists the most-watched streams right now (channel, game, viewers, title).
  - `./clip scan <link|channel|clip|id> [--from] [--to] [--continue] [--judge]` runs one pass.
  - `./clip watch <channel> [--every 15m]` runs a pass every interval until the stream ends.
  - `./clip chat <id> --from --to` shows condensed chat; `./clip transcript` on a stream transcribes the window first.
  - The Studio video page has **Scan new part** and **Scan range** (a `scan` job, then `judge`).
  - The headless judge needs the `claude` CLI logged in (`/login` once in a terminal); without it, passes scan and `/clip <id> --continue` judges in a session.
  - **English only for now:** skip non-English streams/clips (eliasn97's German Icon League) until there is a translation/caption pass. Titles, descriptions and hashtags are always English.
  - **Channels list:** `channels.md` is the operator's living list of target channels (confirmed, suggested, already run), and `channels.json` is the creator list the watchlist reads (`loadCreators`, `src/core/creators.ts`; no file means no creators). Both are the operator's own and gitignored; `channels.example.json` shows the shape. Update `channels.md` after each run; `channel.json` is the posting identity.
  - **Moments first, from a tracker (2026-11-19).** The watchlist works moment by moment, ranked from what the community did: viewer clips over the same seconds and chat laughter (`./clip moments`, `src/scan/moments.ts`; ranking `rankAndStore`, `src/track/rank.ts`). A moment is a region, not a peak second. Two rankings run in a blind trial (`./clip rankers`).
    - **The tracker** (`library/tracking.sqlite`, `src/track/db.ts`) records every stream and moment with its status; wrong content and **sexual moments** (`skipSexual`) are recorded as skipped, not just avoided. `./clip streams` shows it.
    - **The job queue** (`src/track/jobs.ts`): unique per moment, claimed with a lease and fenced writes; a moment job checkpoints its stages (`PIPELINE_STAGES`) and a restart resumes after the last one.
    - **The observer** (`tools/observer.ts`) reads chat over every minute of every tracked stream into a coverage ledger (`src/track/coverage.ts`) and re-ranks each stream every 20 minutes.
    - **Order:** a stream added by hand (`clip track`) first, then the listed creators (`channels.json`), then topics. A moment job works on its own candidates only (`src/pipeline/full.ts`).
    - The full design, every number and why: `docs/operations.md` → "The watchlist"; the spec is `docs/research/stream-pipeline-spec-2026-11-19.md`.
  - **Topics:** `topics.json` lists trending games; every check takes each topic's top 3 eligible streams (`topicPicks`, `src/scan/topics.ts`), and a listed creator never takes a topic's place. **A YouTube stream is never read while live** (`youtubeLive`, `src/ingest/media.ts`): the watchlist waits for the VOD. Details: `docs/operations.md` → "Topics and discovery".
  - **Daily discovery** (SullyGnome, TwitchTracker, Playboard): `docs/operations.md` → "Topics and discovery".
  - **Event streams (sports, award shows, IRL):** use `./clip make <videoId> <candidateId> --no-split`; there is no gameplay facecam, so keep full-frame blur/crop. A viewer clip's `offsetMs` is an anchor near the moment, not a dependable start or end (see `clipSpan`); verify frames around it. For non-English audio, `CLIP_WHISPER_MODEL=small ./clip transcript <id> --from … --to …` uses multilingual Whisper; `small.en` **translates** to English. Turn captions off for music/crowd clips.
- **Judge:** the current workflow is the **in-session agent** judging a video/stream (read seeds, transcript, chat and frames, then `clip candidates add`). No login needed. `./clip judge` defaults to the Claude CLI; `--provider heuristic` (or `CLIP_JUDGE=heuristic`) is the experimental no-LLM shortlist and is not the default. The heuristic reads signals/text, not frames, so review previews before posting.
- **YouTube publishing:** `./clip auth youtube` (OAuth client in `client_secrets.json`, see the example), then `./clip publish <clipId>`; approving in the Studio is what schedules a clip. Uploads are scheduled by default and stored in `exports/<clip>.publish.json` so a retry never double-posts. An 18+ clip goes up private with the age rating read back before it is scheduled. The OAuth app in Testing mode expires logins after 7 days. Setup and details: `docs/operations.md` → "Publishing".
- **TikTok publishing:** `./clip tiktok auth`, then `./clip tiktok upload <clipId>` (an inbox draft the creator finishes) or `tiktok post` (private Direct Post). Setup and limits: `docs/operations.md` → "Publishing".
- **YouTube reach/analytics:** `./clip analytics [--days 28]` pulls views, engaged views, view %, shares and subscribers into `performance.jsonl`; `clip stats` calibrates the scores against them (`docs/operations.md` → "Publishing").
- **Credit:** `./clip credit <clipId> [--text …] [--remove]` sets the on-screen credit (new clips have one already). `channel.json` (next to `library/`) holds the channel's name, the line about what it is, how a creator asks for a removal, `creditOnScreen`, `doNotClip` and the `careful` period (`until`, `why`; see the upload scheduler).
- **Subscribe quick effect:** the Inspector's quick effects include **🔔 Subscribe**, a 3 s subtle text pill near the bottom (the `text` marker, y 0.82, size 38, fade).
- **Worker:** `./clip worker` runs the job queue. It starts by itself whenever anything is queued (`ensureWorker`) and exits when idle.
- `python3 tools/agent-runs.py [--hours 24] [text]` prints what each subagent run cost and whether it read the stills it was shown (from Claude Code's own subagent logs; run it from the project root).
- `npm run typecheck` checks the whole project (TypeScript 7).
- `npm test` runs the offline tests (about 1 s). `npm run test:e2e` runs the slow real-Twitch ones. See **Testing** below.

**Rendering launches headless Chrome**, and so does the worker, which renders. In a sandboxed agent shell, `./clip render`, `./clip studio`, `./clip worker` and anything that queues a render must run with the sandbox disabled, or Chrome dies with "Target closed".
On macOS, renders go through `bin/headless-shell`, which runs Remotion's Chrome Headless Shell with `--in-process-gpu`, because its separate GPU process crashes on macOS 26 (icudtl.dat not found).

## Rules that keep the design working

- **Times:**
  - Markers and clip in/out are **source-video milliseconds**. Never store clip-relative or edited-timeline times.
  - The edited timeline comes only from `buildTimeMap` (`src/core/timeline.ts`). Captions, markers, the Studio playhead and the renderer all map through it, so cut, speed and freeze markers restructure time without moving anything.
- **Marker types:**
  - Every type is a folder in `markers/`. Never special-case a type name in editor, CLI or render code.
  - Types plug in by `layer`:
    - camera → `camera()`
    - overlay/audio → `Component`; an audio type may add `gain()` to turn the clip's own sound down for its span (the `bleep`), read by the composition and by `wordsThatPlay`
    - a camera type may add `filter()`: a CSS filter on the picture for its span (the `mono` black and white), applied to the video only, never to captions or overlays
    - time → `time()`
    - framing → `framing()`
  - Keep `schema.ts` free of React, because the CLI imports it in Node.
- **The channel credits what it clips.** Three mechanisms, none of them optional by accident:
  - The `credit` marker names the creator on screen. In a split layout it sits on the seam between the camera and the game (`seam` in `ClipContext`), near the top otherwise, so it never crosses a face; the editors put the hook line just under the seam (`hookYFor`). The credit is a **source banner**: a platform badge (`▶ YouTube` red / `🎮 Twitch` purple) followed by the exact handle a viewer can type (`youtube.com/@TrashTaste` from `uploader_url`, or `twitch.tv/<login>`). It is `fill: 'clip'` (a marker-type property in `src/markers/define.ts`): it draws for the whole clip whatever its own time, so a trim never leaves it short. `projectFromCandidate` adds it; `setCredit` changes text/label. The render report (`exports/<clip>.json`) records whether the credit was in the picture; QA fails an export without it (`check: 'credit'`), and `publishYoutube` and `uploadTikTokInbox` refuse one (`uncredited`, `src/core/channel.ts`), whatever an older QA report says and even with `--force`. An export from before the rule needs `clip credit` and a render.
  - The post's first line is the expert's context line for the moment, and its "from" line names the chapter the clip is in (`suggestedPost`). The post's `credit` (`creditBlock` in `src/core/post.ts`) is its own field and is ordered for the click: **the full episode/stream at the clip's start first**, then the creator's channel URL, then the title/game, then the channel's disclosure and removal line. `postBody` puts it between the caption and the hashtags, so an edited caption cannot lose it. Everything that posts builds the post with `suggestedPost` (`src/project/index.ts`).
  - `doNotClip` in `channel.json` (`optedOut`, `src/core/channel.ts`): `runPass`, `projectFromCandidate`, `publishYoutube` and `uploadTikTokInbox` refuse, and `--force` does not override it.
- **Never posted (2026-11-22, user: "build change 1" of `docs/research/censorship-review-2026-11-22.md`).** The channel lost two videos to YouTube on 11-21 (a nude shower, a brawl) and its views fell to almost nothing the same day. Three gates, from the start of the pipeline to the end:
  - **The ranker** skips sexual moments (see the tracker above), so the watchlist spends no job on them.
  - **Curation** (`checkDecision`, `riskyCandidates`, `src/curate/index.ts`): a candidate whose moment the experts flagged `sexual` (two kinds of expert), `slur` or `minor` (`unsafeFlags`, `expertFlags` in `src/scan/review.ts`) cannot be `keep` or `review` unless the reason says how the cut leaves it out ("the cut avoids it by …", `AVOIDS`); otherwise it is `drop` on `unsafe`. `pickForEdit` never hands such a candidate to the editor as the fallback `review` pick, and a `keep` only with those words. The queue experts reject `unsafe` when the nude body or the sex scene **is** the moment (`agents/queue-expert.md`).
  - **The publishers** (`neverPost`, `src/qa/unsafe.ts`) refuse, `--force` or not, like a missing credit: nudity the 18+ check is sure of (2+ stills, or one at 0.7+; `AdultCheck.nudity`, older reports by their reason), a slur said in the words that play (`saidSlurs`: the words as heard; a caption fix hides a word, it does not mute it), a `minor` flag on the moment, and a title with a sexual word (`sexualTitleWords`). QA stores the check per render (`unsafe` in `exports/<clip>.qa.json`, a `✖ unsafe` failure the reviewer sees) and the publisher adds what is true now, so only a new render that no longer trips it clears it; the title is read as it is at upload. 18+ does not make any of it postable. `clip unsafe <clips…|--review|--approved> [--look]` lists what would be refused. **Censoring tools (2026-11-22, user: "Yes, change 3"):** captions mask strong swears at the root (`f***ing`, `bulls***`) and star out slurs (`maskSwearing`, `swearIn`, `src/core/risk.ts`; `captions.censor`, on unless turned off, "Mask swears" in the Inspector); the **`bleep`** marker mutes the clip's sound over a span (`gain` 0) and plays a tone (`assets/sfx/bleep.wav`) or nothing, and `./clip censor <clips…> [--swears open|all|none] [--mute] [--propose] [--review]` (`src/edit/censor.ts`) places one over every slur said and the f-words in the first 3 s (word ±80 ms, joined when they touch; source `editor:censor`, replaced on a re-run). A word under a bleep is not said (`wordsThatPlay` → `muted`), so it clears a spoken slur and the careful period's open f-word. The **`blur`** marker blurs or blacks out a box of the 9:16 frame (`backdrop-filter`, checked in a real export) under the captions and the credit. QA measures swearing as a `language` warning, never a failure (`src/qa/language.ts`: heard by kind, bleeped, an f-word in the open, unmasked captions, a swear in the title; `language` in the QA report). The edit skill runs `clip censor` on every clip before the render (step 6.4). Known limits: a `minor` flag is on the moment, so no edit clears it; a slur Whisper mis-hears ("tardy") is not seen; a slur in an on-screen chat is still only a `content` warning.
- **Proposed markers** (`proposed: true`, plus a `source`) are previewed in the Studio but never rendered until accepted.
- **Candidates have two scores.** `score` is quality (is this a good moment at all?); `scores` is the virality funnel (stop, hold, share, standalone, payoff, delivery) and `virality` is its gated 0–100. `rankScore` is 60% virality + 40% signal convergence (`src/candidates/virality.ts`). The weights and post bar are **format-aware**: `podcast-general` and `hot-takes` are talk (a slow open is allowed, `stop` is 15% and not a gate); `stream-moments` is stream (stop-heavy, `stop` 35% and a hard gate). `clip candidates lens` fixes a commentary video that was judged as a stream. A weak context/payoff caps the clip; a hook more than 3 points above its `delivery` is capped at 50. `verdict` is `post`, `recut` or `skip`, and the list puts `post` first. Never rank by the judge's quality score alone; a hook that goes nowhere is worse than no post. The judge supplies `scores` (see `lenses/` and the `/clip` skill); `clip candidates score` can correct them by hand (`--score` sets the quality score; its anchors are in `agents/vocabulary.md`, section 4).
- **One composition** (`src/composition/ClipComposition.tsx`) draws both the preview and the export.
  - Framing is the exception: the renderer frames in ffmpeg for speed, while the Studio does it in CSS (`FramedVideo.tsx`). Change both together.
  - The ffmpeg side is `framingFilter` in `src/render/framing.ts` (pure string building, tested): reframe keyframes become a crop-x expression, and crop/blur/split spans become an overlay with an `enable` expression.
  - Both sides read framing through `framingAt` (`src/markers/define.ts`): a reframe marker sets the mode (crop, blur-fit or split) from its time on.
  - **Split** is facecam on top and the stream below, on a blurred cover of the whole frame (never black). `FramingSchema.split` holds `cam` and `game` boxes in 0–1 source coordinates, `camShare` (the cam band's fraction of the canvas inside the margin) and `content` (`fill` crops the game to cover its band; `fit` shows the whole 16:9 frame at full width, for Just Chatting, websites, IRL and other non-game categories: `contentFitFor`). **All the geometry is `splitGeometry` in `src/core/layout.ts`**, read by the ffmpeg chain, the Studio CSS, the credit's seam and `hookYFor`; change it there. The cam band is sized to the cam panel's shape (`camShareFor`, 0.26–0.42, lower for a small cam so it is never enlarged past `MAX_CAM_ZOOM` 2.6×) and the cam box is cropped into, **never widened** out of (widening pulled the game, a browser or a sponsor logo in beside the face). A panel near the band's shape fills the width, cut around the median face (`fitCamToBand`, so a lean does not leave the band); a much taller or small one is shown whole in a centred window of its own shape on the cover (`camFillsWidth`). `camTopPad` (default 0.04) gives the cam headroom; `canvasPad` (default 0.04) is a margin where the cover shows. `findFacecam` (`src/reframe/cam.ts`) finds a face that keeps returning near a frame edge over the **whole clip**, and `detectSplit` (`src/reframe/split.ts`) applies it with the content fit for the chapter's category (`categoryAt`). `renderClip` cuts the original 16:9 clip first, then resolves a pending split on that clip. `--no-split` keeps the full-frame blur-fit for events/IRL.
  - **Facecam only / game only for a span** (2026-11-23; `solo` marker, `soloGeometry` in `src/core/layout.ts`, read by `FramedVideo.tsx` and `soloChain` in `src/render/framing.ts`): `cam` shows the cam box enlarged up to `MAX_SOLO_ZOOM` (3.5×), at least half its width, full canvas width, centred on the blurred cover; `game` covers the canvas inside the margin with a `fill` split's game box, or shows a `fit` split's whole frame at full width. A span marker whose `framing()` returns `untilEnd` gives a start key (`solo`) and an end key (`restore`); `resolveKeys` (`src/markers/define.ts`, read by `framingAt` and the ffmpeg chain) turns the end into the framing before the span, and drops a span that is not over a split (no facecam found, or the split fell back to blur-fit). The face check reads only split spans; the blur-fit fallback ignores solo keys.
  - **The framed picture is checked before the render** (`checkLayout`, `src/qa/layout.ts`, on `cut.mp4`): any part black for the whole clip (more than 3%, counted only as bars so a dark game corner does not count) and, in a split, a face in the camera band in under 80% of the clip (hybrid detector, 1 sample a second). A whole-clip split with no face in its camera band is switched to blur-fit, saved, and framed again (the split boxes stay for the Studio); black that is in the source (a phone video's sides) is reported, not switched, because blur-fit shows it too; `--keep-layout` and the Studio editor's export only report. The result is `layout` in the render report; QA fails `check: 'layout'` on what is still wrong and warns when it fell back. **The engine owns the layout**: the edit skill and the pipeline prompt tell the editor agent not to change it. Known gap: a split whose band shows *someone else's* face (another player's cam) passes.
  - Face detector: **hybrid is the default** (`CLIP_FACE_DETECTOR=vision|insightface` overrides). Hybrid runs Apple Vision for rectangles and fills frames where Vision misses the face with the InsightFace Python sidecar (`tools/insightface/`). `--detector vision|insightface|hybrid` on `clip split`.
  - **Panel refinement:** `src/reframe/panel.ts` samples 24 frames and snaps the cam box to the border where per-pixel temporal variance steps (webcam room vs game/desktop). It is a Node port of `kiy0ni/auto-video-editor`'s MIT `refine_overlay`; it handles borderless overlays (Hasan) that rectangle detection misses. If no clear border, keep the face/rectangle box.
  - Range tools add a closing reframe at the span's end that restores the framing from before.
- **Footage:**
  - Always go through `resolveMedia` (`src/ingest/media.ts`): a full download or a section, with `offsetMs`.
  - The default is section-only downloads around candidates, which are frame-accurate (tested).
- **Saving a project** goes through `saveProject`, which validates it and bumps `rev`. The Studio watches `clips/` and reloads when another writer bumps `rev`.
- **Edit styles** are data (`src/edit/styles.ts`, browser-safe).
  - autoedit finds the moments: punchlines (the content word before a laugh), the payoff, shouted words, audio shouts, sentence starts and dead air.
  - A style only decides what happens at those moments. Add a style there, not a branch in autoedit.
  - Its random choices (which boom, which emoji) are seeded per clip, so re-running gives the same edit.
- **Queue checkers:** `./clip queues <videoId> --from … --to … --kind meme|viral|clip|all` (`src/scan/queues.ts`, pure core `queuesFrom`, tested on synthetic streams).
  - A queue needs **witnesses**: a chat spike (same definition as `chatAnalysis`), a reaction heard in the audio (`detectReactions`, the tuned thresholds), viewer clips. Fast speech and title words are context and never make a queue.
  - The three differ in their **gate**, not only their weights (`queueGate`): **meme** needs a reaction or chat laughing/gasping/mocking; **viral** needs two witnesses or watched viewer clips; **clip** needs speech plus a witness.
  - Everything is in **moment time**: chat trails the moment 5–25 s, so each 5 s step reads chat from the steps after it. A queue has a stable id (`meme@1:46:25`), `peakMs` = the moment, and an `anchor` (`audio` exact; `clip`, `chat` estimated).
  - **Unknown is not quiet:** `coverageOf` reports missing chat/audio/transcript with the ranges; a blind range with no queues is an error, not an empty list. `--limit` is a view: the output says how many weaker queues it hides. `--transcribe range` transcribes the whole range (plus 2 min either side) once, before several agents read it; `--transcribe regions` only the queue regions and their margins.
  - **Chat is read over every queue's whole region**, spike or not: mood, top messages, doubt (chat calling it fake, staged or AI) and slurs typed (many streams show chat in the frame). A raid, a sub burst or a yes/no vote (`readChat().event`) is busy chat with no moment and is never a witness.
- **The dossier:** `./clip queues <videoId> --from … --to … --kind <kind> --dossier` (`src/scan/dossier.ts`) is what an expert reads. For the next four queues without a verdict, in time order: the evidence, the words (15 s before the region to 25 s after), the read-on (the two minutes after the region: always for a story, for the others when `chatAsksReadOn`), and stills at `dossierTimes` (around an exact moment: −3 s, the moment, +2 s, +6 s; and for every queue the whole region, edge to edge: up to 9 stills, 4 for a story). It prints whose stream it is and the chapter, so verdicts name the creator. `--queue <id>` asks for specific queues. It exists because an agent re-reads its conversation every turn, so its cost is turns × context: the first trial took 269 turns and 323 stills for 20 queues, one command at a time. Change the numbers and `agents/queue-expert.md` together (tested).
- **One detector, one lexicon, one clip span.** Anything that asks "did someone laugh or shout here?" uses `detectReactions` (`src/discover/signals/audio.ts`); never count raw classifier events (most are noise below the thresholds; `booing`, `gasp`, `battle_cry` always are). Every chat word list is `LEXICON` in `src/discover/chatAnalysis.ts` (nine moods; `readChat` also reports raids, sub bursts, copypasta, doubt). A viewer clip's place is `clipSpan` / `clipNearness` (`src/ingest/twitch.ts`): one clip length either side of its offset.
- **Verdicts:** the experts save one per queue with `./clip queue-review <videoId> -` (`src/scan/review.ts`). `checkVerdict` refuses a confirm with no `frame …` line (a story also needs `said: …`, a viral queue a `trigger`), a reject with no ground, and words outside `src/core/vocabulary.ts`. Evidence is checked too: a cited frame must be a still that was fetched (`stillNear`, within 1 s of the cited time), and a `said:` line must be in the transcript within a few minutes of the moment (`findSaid`: four words in a row, compared without spaces; not checked where nothing is transcribed). `clip queue-review --evidence` prints every confirm with the still under each `frame` line and where each `said:` line is, for whoever checks the experts. A confirm needs confidence ≥ 0.5; `cut` says how to cut it and `reason` why. `"found": true` records a moment the checkers have no queue for (it needs `moment`, `start`, `end`; its id is the kind and the moment's time). The story expert reads the range's words once for these, as a step ("After the queues" in its file). Confirmed verdicts within 20 s are one moment (`confirmedMoments`): the most confident leads, the others are second readings. A verdict keeps a snapshot of its queue, so it survives the queue moving or vanishing when the evidence changes (`queueFromVerdict`, `pendingQueues`' 20 s tolerance); `orphanedMoments` finds moments every expert passed on (`other-kind`, or the virality expert's `no-reach`); a merge and a merit reject by another expert count as judged. Saves take a lock: three experts write one ledger.
- **Weighted extraction:** `./clip windows <videoId> --queue <id>` (or `--from … --to … --kind …`) scores start/end windows around a queue — all **15–60 s**, with 300 ms before + 400 ms after included in the clip (the validator's snapping keeps the same buffer). Meme windows put the moment about a third in; 4 s of nothing at the open is a dead open (−15); a region with no words gets windows cut around the moment (`noWords`). With `--queue`, windows are scored around the moment the expert saw (anchor `expert`), the expert's own `start`/`end` is shown scored the same way, and the quote is the line at the hit. `/clip-extractor` adds **one candidate per confirmed moment**; low scores are not dropped at this stage.
- **Candidates:** a candidate cites words (`quote`, checked against the transcript) or is `visual` and has a witness inside it (`evidenceIn`). `kind` and `queueId` record which expert found it. `scoreChecks` adds `score check:` warnings where the transcript contradicts a funnel score, and `spreadNote` says when a batch scored flat. `risk:` warnings (QA shows them to the reviewer) also come from the experts' flags for that moment (`expertRiskWarnings`) from slurs chat typed during the clip (`chatSlurWarnings`, `src/core/risk.ts`), and from chat's doubt (`chatDoubtWarnings`: `DOUBT_MIN` or more chatters calling it fake, staged or AI while it happened; it needs no expert). Bare numbers for `start`/`end` are milliseconds.
- **Curation:** `/clip-curator` assigns a niche, angle and fit and decides `keep` / `review` / `drop`, saved through `./clip curate <videoId> <file>` (`src/curate/`). A drop needs a ground from the vocabulary (a low score is not one), a `removalWeight` and a reason; only `clip curate` changes a candidate's status, and it reports candidates with no decision. A risky moment (sexual from two experts, a slur, a minor) is a drop unless the reason says "the cut avoids it by …" (see "Never posted").
- **Editors:** `./clip editors <clipId> [--meme] [--expert] [--review] [--accept]` writes **proposed markers** (`source: editor:meme` / `editor:expert`). They find the moments first (`clipMetrics`: the reaction, the punch word before it, the quoted line, dead air, comic pauses) and place edits on them; never at a fraction of the clip. They never cut the pause before a laugh, never stack an edit on a marker that is already there (whoever made it), and never change captions or `review.note` (they print notes instead). Re-running replaces that editor's previous markers (taken off before it looks, so its own old premise box does not count as "a hook is there"). **The meme editor's vocabulary** (2026-11-22, `docs/research/meme-edits-2026-11-22.md`, 175 top clip Shorts): a premise box at the open (`text` style `box`), a snap zoom on up to 3 reactions 5 s apart with a `tilt` on shouts, a zoom-out reveal on a silent open (`zoom` `direction: out`), one `tint` beat on a win or affection word, a `counter` on "three, two, one", a time-skip card on a cut of 20 s+, a chat card (`text` style `chat`) with the line chat echoed about the moment (`pickChatLine`, `src/edit/chatCard.ts`: readable, no emotes, nothing from the risk lists, swears masked; placed clear of cuts), and on a fail ending (a loss word in the last 5 s) a `freeze` plus `mono`. In a split (or a clip whose split is found at render, `autoSplit`), the first laugh is a `solo` facecam-only span instead of a zoom, and "look at this / watch this" is a 2 s game-only span (`lookCue`). The rules are pure functions in `src/edit/editors.ts` (`punchReactions`, `failEnding`, `tintBeat`, `countdownIn`, `chatCardAt`), tested in `test/meme-edits.test.ts`.
- **Caption repeats:** a word or a phrase of up to three words said 3+ times in a row is one caption with "X3" on its last word, held to the end of the run (`collapseRepeats`, `src/composition/Captions.tsx`; `captions.repeats`, on unless turned off).
- **Caption looks** (`src/core/captionLooks.ts`; why: `docs/research/edits-research-2026-11-18.md`): `street` (the default: 2–3 word phrases, heavy italic caps with a thick outline, a shouted or exclaimed phrase in yellow as a whole), `commentary` (sentence-case italic, talk clips), `comic` (a colour per phrase, countdowns), `native` (plain, calm clips); `bold`/`boxed`/`clean` are the old auto-captioner looks, kept for clips that use them. A look sets the font, outline, shadow, highlight mode (`word` karaoke, `loud` phrase, `cycle`, `none`), words per page (`splitPages`) and entrance; `CHAR_EM` comes from it. Fonts load in `src/markers/fonts.ts`. The `text` marker has matching `street` (action lines, `*TAKES A BITE*`), `comment` (a viewer's-voice line) and `title` (red title bar) styles; the editors' hook line takes the one that matches the caption look (`hookStyleFor`).
- **Subtitle expert:** `./clip subtitles <clipId>` decides from **what is inside the clip** (its words, tags and reactions) whether captions belong, the look, and word-level fixes. It never turns captions back on once they are off (`--enable` does); `--no-settings` applies word fixes only, for use after a decision made from frames; `--review` is read-only; `--force` overwrites existing word fixes. The skill checks the frames for burned-in captions, facecam/scoreboard collisions, text-heavy screens, music/crowd-only clips and non-English audio, and has the final say.
- **Growing files have one writer.** `transcript.json` and `chat.json` are filled window by window; `ensureTranscribed` and `fetchChat` take `withVideoLock` so a scan, a watcher and several agents cannot lose each other's windows. A gap under 3 s next to transcribed audio is not transcribed (`MIN_TRANSCRIBE_MS`): reading a second past coverage must not start Whisper.
- **A still says which second it is.** Every still has its time in a black strip under the picture (`stillLabelFilter`, drawn with boxes as a seven-segment display: this ffmpeg has no text filter). An agent gets a batch of stills back as bare pictures and used to match them to their times by position; 2 of 11 checked `frame` lines were wrong that way. Labelled stills are named `<ms>_<width>.jpg`; older plain `<ms>.jpg` stills stay on disk for old verdicts and are never handed out again.
- **A still is evidence.** `grabFrames` (`src/ingest/frames.ts`) takes stills from local footage when it exists (exact) and replaces any cached still older than that footage; stills for different seconds that come back byte-identical are refetched once and then dropped with a warning; the width is part of the cache key. `clip frames` prints `(no still)` rather than a path it cannot trust.
- **Docs that state engine numbers are tested.** `test/lenses.test.ts` fails when a lens file's weights, gates or post bar differ from `FORMAT_RULES`, or when the agent docs use a term `clip vocab` does not have. Change them together.
- **Titles:** viral clip channels use a short nickname, a present/past-tense action, one all-caps word, and 1–2 reaction emojis, usually under 60 characters with no colon. `suggestTitles` now emits five options: bold name+action (default), quote, curiosity (“No way …”), chat-aware, and the searchable context. The research sample is in `.cache/shorts-titles.json` and `.cache/streamer-shorts.txt`.
- **Signals:**
  - Each signal is a self-contained file in `src/discover/signals/`, registered in the `SIGNALS` list.
  - Candidate `signals` and `why` are computed by the engine, never written by the judge.
- **Streams are partial.** Twitch has no captions, comments or heatmap, so chat, viewer clips, audio and the transcript exist only where scanned.
  - **Viewer clips accumulate** (`mergeViewerClips`, `src/ingest/chat.ts`): a refresh is a page of the channel's top clips filtered to one broadcast, so a newer broadcast can push an older one's off the page. A refresh merges by slug (a clip seen once stays, views only go up, an exact offset beats an estimate) and logs how many came back (`fetches`); it never empties the file.
  - Record what's been fetched as ranges (`fetched`, `covered`) and treat the rest as unknown, never as quiet.
  - Merge new data per range (`mergeChat`, `mergeTranscript`, `analyzeAudioChunk`). A transcript merge renumbers words, so it goes through `remapCaptionFixes`.
  - Passes overlap by 2 min and stop 3 min short of a live edge (`planPass`, tested).
  - Twitch's chat replay pages by time offset only: cursor paging needs a browser integrity token.
- **YouTube is flaky.**
  - yt-dlp runs with curl-cffi impersonation.
  - Captions are retried on HTTP 429, and download jobs retry transient 403/429/5xx errors (`worker.ts`).
  - A section without a video stream is rejected.
- **Word numbers are ids.** Caption fixes are keyed by word number. Any change to transcript parsing must go through ingest, which remaps fixes (`remapCaptionFixes`).
- **Long work is a job.** Add a `JobKind` (`src/jobs/queue.ts`), a handler in `src/pipeline/handlers.ts` and a limit in `LIMITS` (`src/jobs/worker.ts`); batches chain through `src/pipeline/batch.ts`.
- **Layers:** every `src/` folder has a place in `LAYERS` (`test/layers.test.ts`, lowest first: core, jobs, markers, project, composition, ingest, discover, scan, extract, candidates, review, curate, reframe, qa, edit, render, judge, track, pipeline, publish, studio, cli, tools). A file imports only from its own folder or one below; dynamic `import()` counts. The test fails on an import that goes up, which is what keeps the folders free of cycles: move the code down, or pass it in from above (the worker loop takes its handlers as an argument). A new folder needs a place in `LAYERS`.
- **Files:** write JSON with `writeJson` (temp file + rename) so watchers never read half a file.
- **Settings:** read an environment variable only through `setting()` (`src/core/config.ts`), which parses it strictly (`15m` for a number is an error, not NaN). A new one goes in `SETTINGS` with its default and a line on what it does, and in the table in `docs/operations.md` → "Settings" (`test/config.test.ts` checks both and fails on a direct `process.env` read). `./clip config` prints the values in force.

## Testing

- **`npm test` (offline, about 1 s; run it after every change).** Node's test runner through tsx, no extra dependencies.
  - Pure logic: time, the time map, framing (blur/crop/split filter strings and facecam detection), candidate virality/ranking (talk vs stream), hook/payoff QA, ranges, link parsing, chat spikes and the chat lexicon, pass planning, queue checkers on synthetic streams (`test/queues.test.ts`), the dossier's stills and words (`test/dossier.test.ts`), the credit and the do-not-clip list (`test/credit.test.ts`), extraction windows, editor placement, the subtitle decision.
  - On made-up videos in the sandbox: the candidate validator (`test/candidates.test.ts`: the buffer, visual candidates, score checks), the two ledgers and the file lock (`test/ledgers.test.ts`), the editors and subtitles applied to a clip (`test/editors.test.ts`).
  - Doc-sync: `test/lenses.test.ts` checks the lens files and agent docs against the engine, that the delegated roles all name one model, and that the expert protocol describes the dossier the engine builds.
  - Integration with only the network faked: `test/scan.test.ts` runs passes over a **simulated live stream**. The broadcast grows between passes, chat has known moments, a pass comes too early, and the stream ends. The real planning, merging, spike detection, seed picking, windowed transcripts and scan.json run against it.
  - Tests import `test/helpers/sandbox.ts` first: it points `CLIP_DATA` at a temp folder so the real library/, clips/ and exports/ are never touched.
- **`npm run test:e2e` (real Twitch, a few minutes).** Checks every web API query the tool uses, then scans an 8-minute range of a recent ended broadcast with real yt-dlp, ffmpeg, the audio classifier and Whisper. Run it when touching Twitch or the scan path; Twitch can change its API without notice. `E2E_TWITCH_VOD=<number>` pins a broadcast.
- **By hand, what tests can't see:**
  - The Studio in the browser pane: the candidates page, the editor, the stream scan panel.
  - A real render and its QA.
  - `clip watch <channel> --every 3m --no-judge` on a channel that's live (passes on a growing broadcast).
- **Edge cases covered:**
  - Queues: a laugh and the chat spike it caused are one queue on the laugh; chat alone places the moment before the spike; classifier noise; fast talk with no witness; a raid; a yes/no vote; a muted stretch; an unscanned range; a spike's tail; stable ids; chat doubt and slurs with no spike; which side of a viewer clip's offset to look.
  - Ledgers: a confirm with no frame, a frame nobody fetched, a quote that is not in the transcript, a word outside the vocabulary, a found moment, a queue that moved, a moment both experts passed on, a drop with no ground or for a low score, a reversed drop, an already-made clip.
  - Editors and captions: effects on the punch word and the reaction, the pause before a laugh, a scream as the open, a reviewer note and a captions-off decision left alone.
  - Chat spikes: spammers (distinct chatters), bots, a stream that slowly gets busier, gaps in fetched chat, a spike at a range start, "clip it" requests.
  - Passes: too early, the stream ending, a start past the end, `--to` past what exists, overlapping passes, a concurrent scan (lock), a stale lock.
  - Transcripts: caption fixes surviving a merge.
  - Twitch API: cursor refused, a page full of one second, chat turned off, a missing broadcast, clip offsets.
  - Times: rounding at :59.95.

## Measured baselines (Apple M1, 16 GB)

- **`clip ingest`:** metadata and comments take about 30 s for a 2 h episode. The audio job (download + laugh detection) takes about 1 min more.
- **Footage:** sections download at about real-time speed, 6 in parallel. A 2-minute section is about 20 MB and takes about 80 s; the full video would be 1.6–4.4 GB.
- **Audio events:** about 18 s per hour of audio. At a 0.5 threshold, 82% of caption laughs are matched (per-minute correlation 0.87).
- **Faces:** about 2.3 s per minute of 24 fps video, 5.4 s per minute at 60 fps.
- **`clip discover`:** under 1 s.
- **`clip render`:** 1.5–2× the clip length.
  - A 55 s clip took 95–113 s.
  - Settings: 6 tabs, 4 video-decode threads, VideoToolbox encoding, then loudness normalised to −14 LUFS.
  - Tuning knobs: `CLIP_CONCURRENCY`, `CLIP_OVT`, `CLIP_HW=0`, `CLIP_GL`, `CLIP_BROWSER`, `CLIP_LOG=verbose`.
- **Judge:** the headless judge (`claude -p`) uses your Claude Code usage. `CLIP_CLAUDE` overrides which binary is used.
- **Twitch (xQc, 2026-11-07):**
  - Chat replay: about 10 minutes of chat in 11 s, 4 slices in parallel.
  - Section downloads: about 3× real time (20 s of 720p60 in 6 s).
  - A 15-minute scan pass: about 55 s, then about 45 s to transcribe 8 minutes around its seeds.
- **Whisper:** small.en takes about 0.12× real time (2 h in about 14 min), with words matching YouTube's about 85% of the time. It lives in `.cache/whisper` (about 600 MB). The multilingual `small` model is 488 MB; `-l en` in whisper.cpp **translates** non-English audio to English, so `CLIP_WHISPER_MODEL=small` gives readable English words for German streams but not German captions.
